# Customer Support Reply Writer

A good support reply does three things: proves the human read the ticket, answers or clearly states the path, and sets expectations.

## Steps

1. Answer the actual question first — if the answer exists, it is the first line, not paragraph three. Then add context. Burying the answer to "sound helpful" wastes the customer's time.
2. Mirror their language (Thai or English), their formality level, and their terms for the product. If they say "the billing page", you say "the billing page", not "InvoiceHub module".
3. Show you read the specifics: reference their order number, their error, their earlier message. One specific detail does more for trust than three apologies.
4. Bad news first when it is bad: cannot refund, feature unavailable, ETA slipped. State it plainly, give the reason in one line, then pivot to what you CAN do. Never make the customer excavate the no from a wall of text.
5. When unresolved: name the exact next step, who owns it, and when they will hear back ("our engineering team is checking; I'll update you by tomorrow 17:00 or sooner"). Then actually update at that time even if the update is "still investigating".
6. Ask for what you need explicitly and minimally: one screenshot, one piece of info — batch your questions into a single numbered list instead of a slow tennis match.
7. Close with their next action and an easy escalation path. No canned "let us know if you have any other questions" when the case is clearly still open — it reads as dismissal.

## Checks

- The answer (or the state of the investigation) is in the first two lines.
- Any promise made has an owner and a time.
