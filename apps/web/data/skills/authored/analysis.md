# SWOT Analysis Builder

A SWOT is only useful when each point is specific, evidenced, and tied to an action. Generic word salads are worthless.

## Steps

1. Define the unit of analysis precisely: the whole company, one product, one market entry, or one competitor. State it at the top.
2. Collect evidence before opinions: performance numbers, customer feedback, market data. If given none, mark points as "hypothesis — needs data" rather than stating them as facts.
3. Fill the four quadrants with 3-5 specific points each:
   - Strengths: internal capabilities that genuinely differentiate (say why they are hard to copy).
   - Weaknesses: internal gaps with real consequences ("no recurring revenue" not "could be better").
   - Opportunities: external shifts the unit can actually act on.
   - Threats: external risks with likely impact and timing.
4. For every point, attach the evidence or assumption behind it in parentheses.
5. Derive implications, not just lists: for each quadrant, end with "so what" — one action or decision it suggests.
6. Rank the final 3 highest-leverage moves that combine quadrants (e.g. use strength S1 to capture opportunity O2).
7. Note what data would most reduce uncertainty in the weakest quadrant.

## Output

Table for the quadrants, then "Top 3 implications" as a short list.
