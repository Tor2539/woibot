# Presentation Slide Designer

Slides are props for the speaker, not documents to read. Design each slide to be understood in 4 seconds.

## Steps

1. Write the narrative first, as a sequence of one-sentence claims ("the market grew, but our share fell"). Each claim becomes one slide; the slide title IS the claim, not a topic label.
2. Choose the evidence type per slide: a number (make it huge), a chart (previously cleaned), a comparison table (max 3 columns), a screenshot (cropped to the point), or an image (relevant, not decorative).
3. Apply the 4-second rule: title states the takeaway, body carries only what supports it, everything else moves to appendix or speaker notes.
4. Text discipline: no full paragraphs; bullets of max 8-10 words; font sizes that survive the back row (titles ~28pt+, body ~18pt+ on standard slides).
5. Charts: one chart one message; highlight the series or point that proves the title, grey out the rest; label directly; state the source and period.
6. Consistency: one layout grid, two fonts max, palette fixed across the deck; alignment is exact, not approximate.
7. Build the arc: opening slide with the question or stakes, evidence middle, decision or ask at the end. Include an agenda only for decks over 15 slides.
8. Read the deck titles in sequence — they must tell the whole story by themselves. If they do not, reorder or merge slides.

## Checks

- Every slide passes the 4-second test.
- The ask is explicit on the final content slide.
