# Performance Review Writer

A review is evidence + trajectory, not a personality verdict. Specific examples over adjectives; future focus over autopsy.

## Steps

1. Gather evidence across the full period: goals set, deliverables with dates, peer feedback, metrics, and incidents with context. Recency bias is the default failure — weight the whole period.
2. For each review area, write the claim with a supporting example: "Reliability: shipped the billing migration on schedule and handled the cutover with zero downtime (March)" — claim without example reads as opinion.
3. Balance honestly: 2-3 strengths with evidence, 1-2 growth areas with evidence. All-positive reviews waste the format; all-negative ones destroy trust. Growth areas get described as specific behaviors to change, not character labels ("runs large meetings without an agenda" not "poor communicator").
4. Distinguish impact from effort: recognize effort in growth areas, tie ratings to impact. State what the difference means for the next period concretely.
5. For missed goals, include context fairly: what changed, what the person controlled, what they did about it. Same for over-achievement — note whether goals were sandbagged.
6. Write the forward section: 2-3 priorities for next period, the support or resources committed, and the skill to develop. This is the part that changes next year's review.
7. Calibrate language: avoid absolutes ("never", "always") unless documented; avoid comparisons to named colleagues; keep tone factual and respectful — the person will reread this more than once.
8. Check before finalizing: would the person recognize every example? Is anything here a surprise? Surprises in a review mean the feedback loop failed earlier — note it as a process fix.
