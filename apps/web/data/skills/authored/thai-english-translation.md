# Thai-English Business Translation

Translation preserves intent, not words. Register (formal/casual), politeness level, and cultural framing must survive the trip.

## Steps

1. Identify the document type, audience, and purpose before translating; the target register follows: business emails stay polite-neutral, marketing may adapt more freely, contract language stays conservative and literal.
2. Translate meaning units, not words: Thai sentences often omit subjects and use particles for tone; English needs explicit subjects. Conversely, English passives and legal hedges need natural Thai equivalents, not word order mimicry.
3. Handle Thai politeness markers (ครับ/ค่ะ/นะคะ): in English, carry politeness through phrasing ("would you kindly", "we appreciate") rather than literal insertion; in Thai→English formal letters, mirror the closing formula conventions.
4. Numbers, dates, and names: preserve exact figures; Thai Buddhist-era dates convert to CE with a note; keep honorifics and titles consistent with the target convention (Khun → Mr/Ms or keep "Khun" if the audience knows Thai context).
5. Culture-bound items (food, administrative terms, units): translate + brief gloss on first use ("som tam (papaya salad)"), keep official Thai terms with romanization where no equivalent exists.
6. Preserve formatting exactly: tables, bullet structure, line breaks, emphasis. The translated document should be drop-in usable.
7. Produce a translator's note listing: items you interpreted, ambiguity in the source ("the source's ค่ะ suggests a female speaker — tone adapted"), and any place meaning cannot be fully carried. Notes prevent downstream misunderstandings.
8. Self-review the next day or with fresh eyes: read only the target text — does it read as if originally written in that language?

## Checks

- No figure, date, or name altered.
- Register matches the document's purpose.
