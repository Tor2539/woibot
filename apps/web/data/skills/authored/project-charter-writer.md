# Project Charter Writer

The charter is the cheapest moment to fix a project. Ambiguity here compounds into every later meeting.

## Steps

1. State the problem and why now, in the sponsor's terms: the pain, its measurable cost, and the trigger. A project without a quantified pain collects requirements forever.
2. Define objectives as outcomes with numbers: "reduce invoice processing time from 5 days to 1 by Q3". Objectives are the yardstick for every later scope argument.
3. Write scope as three lists: in scope (specific deliverables), out of scope (explicit exclusions — the list that saves the project), and dependencies on other teams with named contacts.
4. Name the roles: sponsor (removes blockers, approves), owner (day-to-day accountability), core team with allocation %, and advisory stakeholders. Unnamed roles cause rework when decisions stall.
5. Set milestones with dates and one acceptance criterion each; mark which milestone is the "no-return" point (budget spent, contract signed) and what decision it triggers.
6. List top 5 risks with likelihood, impact, and mitigation owner; include at least one "assumption that would sink us if wrong" and how it will be validated early.
7. Define success measurement: metric, baseline, target, measurement method, and review date. Also define the stop condition — what result means the project should stop or pivot.
8. Keep to one-two pages, get explicit sign-off from sponsor and owner, and version it: charters change through formal change notes, not hallway agreement.

## Checks

- Out-of-scope list exists and is specific.
- Every milestone has a date and acceptance criterion.
