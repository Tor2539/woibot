# Keyword Research Plan

Keywords are stand-ins for people with questions. Cluster them by the question asked, then answer each cluster with one page.

## Steps

1. Start from business value, not search volume: which queries, if you ranked, would bring people who buy or convert? List seed topics from your product, customer questions, and sales objections.
2. Expand each seed into question variants, comparison terms ("X vs Y", "best X for Z"), and problem phrasings. Use autocomplete, related searches, and customer language from support tickets — real phrasing beats thesaurus guessing.
3. Classify intent per keyword: informational (learn), commercial (compare), transactional (act), navigational (find a site). Mixed-intent clusters get split, because one page cannot win two different intents.
4. Cluster keywords that a single page could satisfy together (same question, variants of phrasing). Each cluster = one candidate page; overlapping clusters get merged.
5. Estimate difficulty honestly: look at who currently ranks (big brands? weak forums?) and your site's authority relative to them. Mark clusters as quick-win, medium-term, or aspiration.
6. Score clusters by value: estimated traffic potential x relevance to the business (a buying-intent cluster with modest volume often beats a huge informational one).
7. Map clusters to pages: existing page to improve vs new page to create, with the target keyword, format (guide, comparison, tool, category), and the internal links it will receive.
8. Output a prioritized list: cluster, keywords, intent, difficulty, value, page action — ordered by value/difficulty ratio, first 3 marked for immediate work.
