# Source Credibility Checker

A source is credible for a claim, not in general. A vendor's blog may be fine for feature lists and useless for market share.

## Steps

1. Establish provenance: who published this, on what platform, and is the original source found (not a repost or syndication)? Trace to the primary document where possible.
2. Check authorship: named and identifiable, with relevant credentials or track record? Anonymous or organizational voice without a named expert lowers confidence for factual claims.
3. Check the evidence behind the claim: does it cite data, methods, or documents? Are the numbers internally consistent (percentages, totals, sample sizes)? Uncited assertion = opinion, whatever the byline.
4. Check motive and funding: who benefits from this framing? Ads, sponsored content, advocacy positions, or a product being reviewed all matter. Declare the conflict, do not just discount silently.
5. Check currency: publication date and whether the claim is time-sensitive (prices, market data, regulations). Undated pages get treated as stale by default.
6. Cross-check the core claim against one independent source type. Agreement raises confidence; disagreement goes into the report as a conflict, not a tie-break.
7. Produce the verdict: rating (strong / usable with caveats / weak) and a usage note — exactly which claims this source may support and which it may not, with the reason.
