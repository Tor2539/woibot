# Pricing Strategy Analysis

Price is a positioning decision with numbers attached. Analyze value first, competitors second, costs last.

## Steps

1. Quantify the value delivered: what does the customer gain (revenue up, cost down, time saved, risk removed) and in what currency? Price anchored to a fraction of delivered value is easier to defend than cost-plus.
2. Gather willingness-to-pay signals: current conversion at existing price, win/loss reasons, price objections in sales notes, survey data if available. Mark what is evidence versus assumption.
3. Collect competitor anchors: price points, models (flat, per-seat, usage), packaging boundaries, and what their top tier signals. Position deliberately: premium, parity, or penetration — with a reason.
4. Choose the model before the number: flat vs per-seat vs usage vs hybrid. Per-seat rewards account growth; usage scales with delivered value; flat simplifies the sale. State the revenue math under each.
5. Design 3 tiers (if tiering): good / better / best where the middle tier is the intended destination; each tier boundary answers a real customer difference, not feature trivia.
6. Compute unit economics at the proposed prices: margin per unit, break-even volume, revenue at current and projected volumes.
7. Plan the rollout as a test: grandfathering policy for existing customers, the metric that will judge success (revenue per lead, conversion, churn), review date, and the script for communicating the change.
8. Write the recommendation with the top three risks and the evidence that would reverse it.

## Checks

- Value logic stated before competitor copying.
- Rollout has a success metric and a review date.
