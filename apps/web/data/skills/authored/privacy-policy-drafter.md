# Privacy Notice and Disclaimers Drafter

Draft in plain language, collect only what is real, and mark every point where local law requires counsel. This produces a draft for legal review — never a final legal document.

## Steps

1. Inventory the data actually collected: each field, its purpose, where it is stored, who can see it, and how long it is kept. The notice must match this table exactly — aspirational notices are themselves a compliance risk.
2. For each data item, write the purpose and the legal basis placeholder (consent, contract necessity, legitimate interest — as advised by counsel). No purpose, no collection: recommend cutting fields that serve no stated purpose.
3. Draft the notice sections in this order: what we collect, why, who we share it with (name processors: payments, analytics, hosting), retention period per category, user rights (access, correction, deletion, export — adjust to jurisdiction with counsel), how to exercise rights, contact, and update policy.
4. Write at B1-B2 reading level: short sentences, concrete nouns, zero legalese except terms counsel requires.
5. Disclaimers: for content products, draft the "not professional advice" line (financial/medical/legal) with the specific profession named; for estimates or calculations, state assumptions and that results are indicative.
6. Terms-of-use outline: acceptable use, accounts and suspension, IP ownership, limitation of liability placeholder, governing law placeholder.
7. Mark every jurisdiction-dependent item with [LEGAL REVIEW REQUIRED] and produce a summary list of those items for the lawyer — this is the deliverable's main value.

## Checks

- Notice matches the data inventory one-to-one.
- All jurisdiction-sensitive placeholders flagged, none invented.
