# Job Description Writer

Job descriptions filter applications. Vague ones attract broad mismatch; honest, specific ones shorten the funnel.

## Steps

1. Start from the role's reason to exist: what problem does this hire solve in the next 6-12 months? Write the top 3 outcomes the person must achieve ("ship the new checkout flow", "grow APAC pipeline from 20 to 60 SQLs/month").
2. Convert outcomes into responsibilities with realistic time weights (40% X, 30% Y...). Cut responsibilities no one would spend >5% of their time on — they inflate the role.
3. Split requirements honestly into must-have (would disqualify a candidate if missing — max 4-5) and nice-to-have (teachable). Every extra must-have shrinks the pool, especially for candidates who self-filter.
4. Name the team, reporting line, and how the role collaborates (who they work with daily). Candidates weigh context heavily; anonymity signals dysfunction.
5. State the concrete process: stages, expected timeline, what each stage assesses. Also name salary range or "range available on request" per your policy — opacity costs qualified applicants.
6. Describe what the person will learn or gain; senior candidates read growth signals, not perks.
7. Language check: avoid internal jargon and coded requirements ("rockstar", "young and energetic"); use plain titles that match how candidates actually search (align with market naming even if your org chart differs).
8. Read once as a candidate: would a strong, busy person apply within 5 minutes of reading? If the value proposition is unclear, rewrite the first paragraph.
