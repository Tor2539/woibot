# Competitor Analysis Framework

Analyze competitors to make a decision, not to fill a slide. Start from the decision.

## Steps

1. State the decision this analysis serves (e.g. "should we enter the mid-market?"). Every section must inform it.
2. Pick 3-5 direct competitors plus 1-2 adjacent ones. For each, gather what is actually verifiable: public pricing, product pages, reviews, job postings, changelogs.
3. For each competitor, fill a standard profile:
   - Positioning: who they claim to serve and with what promise (use their own words).
   - Offer: core product, add-ons, what is deliberately missing.
   - Pricing: model, tiers, packaging tricks, discount signals.
   - Channels: where they sell and how they acquire (site, marketplaces, partners).
   - Moat: what protects them — brand, switching costs, data, scale, network effects — or "none visible".
4. Build a comparison table on the 4-6 attributes that matter for the decision. Mark unknown cells explicitly as unknown; do not guess.
5. Identify gaps: segments underserved, promises competitors cannot keep, features everyone bundles but customers complain about (use reviews as evidence).
6. End with 2-4 differentiation moves, each tied to a competitor weakness and a customer pain, with the first verification step for each.

## Checks

- Every factual claim traces to a named source.
- The recommendation connects to the stated decision, not generic advice.
