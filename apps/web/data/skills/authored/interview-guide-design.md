# Structured Interview Guide

Unstructured interviews feel great and predict little. Structure = same questions, same rubric, evidence over vibes.

## Steps

1. Derive 3-5 competencies from the job's outcomes (e.g. technical depth, stakeholder communication, ownership). Each competency gets at least one dedicated interviewer — coverage, not repetition.
2. For each competency, write questions with follow-up probes that dig for evidence: behavioral past ("tell me about a time X — what did you do, what happened") and situational future ("how would you approach X here"). Prepare the probes in advance so pressure does not produce leading questions.
3. Define scoring anchors before the interview: what a 1, 3, 5 answer actually sounds like per competency ("5 = quantified result + reflection on failure; 1 = vague claims, no specifics"). Interviewers score against anchors, not against each other's presence.
4. Assign stages: who asks what, in what order, and the handoff rule (later interviewers do not re-ask; they drill gaps from the debrief notes).
5. Ban the known noise: no puzzles unrelated to the job, no questions about protected characteristics, no "culture fit" without a defined behavior behind it.
6. Build the debrief form: per competency, score, one quoted piece of evidence, and one open question. Debriefs start with independent scoring to avoid anchoring on the most senior voice.
7. After each hire, review which questions and scores actually predicted performance; revise the guide. Structure is a living system, not a formality.
