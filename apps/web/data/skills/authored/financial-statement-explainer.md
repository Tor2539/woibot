# Financial Statement Explainer

Translate statements into decisions. The reader needs "is this business healthy and what should we watch", not an accounting lecture.

## Steps

1. Orient the reader: three statements, three jobs — P&L (profitability over a period), balance sheet (what is owned/owed at a point in time), cash flow (cash actually moving). State the period and currency up front.
2. Walk the P&L top down: revenue, gross margin, operating expenses, operating profit, net profit. Explain each as a rate or relationship ("gross margin 62% means 62 satangs per baht of sales remain for operating costs"), not as jargon.
3. Read the balance sheet for structure: current assets vs current liabilities (short-term cushion), debt vs equity mix, and anything unusual (large receivables = sales booked but uncollected).
4. Read cash flow as the truth serum: operating cash flow vs net profit — profit without operating cash is a question, not a triumph. Note investing and financing directions.
5. Compute the 6 numbers that matter for most decisions: revenue growth %, gross margin %, operating margin %, current ratio, debt-to-equity, operating cash flow. Present as a small table with prior period for comparison.
6. Flag anomalies with a plain-language hypothesis and what document would confirm it ("receivables doubled — check for one large unpaid deal or a term change").
7. Answer the actual question posed (can we afford X? is the business investable?) using only computed numbers; label what the statements cannot answer.

## Checks

- Every ratio explained in one plain sentence.
- Profit-vs-cash tension explicitly addressed.
