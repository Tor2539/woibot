# Meeting Minutes Writer

Good minutes are scannable by someone who missed the meeting and answer only three questions: what was decided, who does what, what is still open.

## Steps

1. Collect the raw material: notes, transcript, chat log, agenda. Note the meeting date, attendees, and purpose at the top.
2. Separate content into four buckets as you read:
   - Decisions (what was agreed, including any dissent noted)
   - Action items (task, owner, deadline)
   - Open questions (unresolved, with who is expected to resolve them)
   - Discussion highlights (one line each, only if context matters)
3. Write action items as "Owner: verb + object + deadline", e.g. "Mina: send revised quote to vendor by Tue". If no owner was named, write "[unassigned]" — do not invent one.
4. Mark decisions that were made without full agreement as "provisional" rather than smoothing them over.
5. Format: header block (date, attendees, purpose), then Decisions, Action Items, Open Questions, Notes. Keep the whole thing under one page when possible.
6. Close with the date and topic of the next meeting if scheduled.

## Checks

- Every commitment has an owner and a date (or an explicit gap marker).
- Someone absent could act on this without asking a follow-up question.
