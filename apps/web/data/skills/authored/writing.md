# Business Document Writer

Write documents a busy reader can act on: short, structured, and honest about what is known versus unknown.

## Steps

1. Restate the goal in one sentence and identify the audience (decision-maker, reviewer, or general staff). Tone and depth follow from this.
2. Outline first, draft second: title, one-paragraph executive summary, 3-5 body sections, closing next steps. If the outline is weak, fix it before writing prose.
3. Put the conclusion in the executive summary. Every section after it should support, not surprise.
4. One idea per section, with a heading that states the takeaway ("Costs concentrate in logistics", not "Costs").
5. Separate fact from judgment: state facts plainly, and label estimates or assumptions explicitly, e.g. "assumes current pricing holds".
6. Use placeholders in [square brackets] for any number, name, or date that was not provided. Never invent statistics.
7. Close with next steps that have owners and dates where possible, even if the owner is "[assign]".
8. Edit once for length (cut 20%) and once for clarity (read every paragraph's first sentence in sequence; it should summarize the document).

## Checks

- Can a reader get the full picture from summary + headings alone?
- Are all numbers either sourced or bracketed placeholders?
- Does every section earn its place?
