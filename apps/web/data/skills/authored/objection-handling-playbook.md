# Objection Handling Playbook

An objection is information, not a wall. The playbook's job is to turn the common ones into documented, evidence-backed responses the whole team can use.

## Steps

1. Collect the real objections from recorded calls, lost-deal notes, and CRM — ranked by frequency. The top 5-8 get playbook entries; invented objections waste training time.
2. For each objection, separate the stated concern from the underlying one: "too expensive" often means "not yet convinced of value" or "budget cycle timing". Add a clarifying move: "happy to talk price — so I point at the right value: what are you comparing us to?"
3. Write the response pattern per objection: acknowledge (one line, no defensiveness), reframe with evidence (a metric, a case with numbers, a risk comparison), and confirm ("does that address the concern, or is there another piece?").
4. Categorize each objection as value, timing, trust, authority, or fit — different categories need different evidence, and the category tells you which proof to reach for.
5. Mark the no-path objections honestly: when the fit truly is wrong, the play is a graceful referral or a smaller-start option — protecting future trust beats forcing a bad deal.
6. For "need to ask my boss/committee": provide the one-pager that helps them sell internally (problem, options, costs, risks of status quo) and offer to join the internal discussion.
7. Rehearse with role-play: 10 minutes per objection, rotating roles monthly; record the best phrasings heard in real calls and fold them back into the playbook.
8. Version and date the playbook; stale evidence ("our case study from 3 years ago") undermines the moment it is spoken.
