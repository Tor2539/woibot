# User and Stakeholder Interview Guide

People predict badly and remember selectively. Ask about past behavior and specifics, not hypotheticals and averages.

## Steps

1. Write the learning goal: 2-3 things you genuinely do not know, phrased as questions the interview could answer ("why do new users abandon setup at step 3?").
2. Open with warm-up facts that establish context without judgment: role, situation, when they last did the relevant task. Two minutes, closed questions only.
3. Build question ladders: start concrete ("walk me through the last time you did X, step by step"), then dig into moments ("what happened right after step 2?"), then reasons ("what made that the right choice?"). Abstractions come last.
4. Ban hypothetical questions ("would you use...?") and prediction questions ("how often would you...?") — replace with past behavior and specifics. If you must test a concept, show it and ask what they would change, not whether they like it.
5. Prepare probes that don't lead: "tell me more", "what did you expect to happen?", "you mentioned X twice — what's behind that?".
6. Control your biases: do not demonstrate the product while asking for independent recall; do not nod harder at ideas you like; note contradicting evidence on purpose.
7. End with two questions always: "what should I have asked but didn't?" and "who else should I talk to?".
8. Debrief within an hour: facts, quotes with context, and the 2-3 insights — memory of interviews decays fast.
