# Onboarding Plan Builder

Onboarding failure is rarely about documents; it is about unclear expectations and missing social connection. Plan both.

## Before day 1

1. Logistics checklist: accounts and hardware ready and tested, calendar invites sent, first-week schedule shared, and the manager's welcome message drafted. Access failures on day 1 cost weeks of momentum.
2. Assign an onboarding buddy (peer, not manager) and a mentor for craft questions; name both to the new hire.

## Weeks 1-2: orient and connect

3. First-week schedule: short intro meetings with key collaborators (each 25 minutes, with "what does your team need from this role" as the standing question), tool walkthroughs, and reading list in priority order (max 5 items — long lists get ignored).
4. Give one small, shippable task by day 3-5 that touches the real workflow; early contribution beats early theory.

## Days 30-60: contribute with support

5. Set 30-day goals: own 2-3 real tasks end to end with a named reviewer. Success = delivered with feedback, not solo perfection.
6. 60-day goals: first independent deliverable or process improvement; give the new hire a map of the team's recurring work and where they now own a piece.

## Days 90: full ownership

7. 90-day goals: full ownership of a defined area, plus a retro conversation: what in the onboarding actually helped, what was noise (feed back into this plan).
8. Checkpoints at days 7, 30, 60, 90 with the manager: a fixed 3-question agenda (what's clear, what's blocking, what to adjust). Document outcomes lightly; patterns across hires reveal systemic gaps.
