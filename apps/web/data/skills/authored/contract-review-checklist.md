# Contract Review Checklist (Non-Lawyer Basics)

Business review catches the deal-shaped risks; legal review catches the law-shaped ones. Do yours first, in writing.

## Steps

1. Confirm the parties' legal names match reality (registered names, not brand names) and that the signer has authority. A contract with the wrong entity is a problem before any clause is read.
2. Scope: what exactly is delivered, by when, with what acceptance criteria? Vague deliverables ("support as needed") become disputes. Rewrite the vague ones as measurable terms and propose them.
3. Money: total cost including fees, taxes, expenses, and escalation clauses; payment triggers (on signing? on milestone? on acceptance?) and late-payment terms both directions.
4. Term and termination: length, renewal (auto-renew with what notice window?), and exit rights for convenience or cause, including what happens to deliverables and prepayments on exit.
5. Liability: caps, exclusions, and indemnities. Note anything uncapped or one-sided (we indemnify them broadly; they cap at pocket change). Flag for legal, do not negotiate alone.
6. IP and data: who owns deliverables, background IP, and — critically — your data during and after the term. Exit must include data return in a usable format and deletion certificate.
7. Confidentiality and non-solicit: duration and whether they are mutual.
8. Compliance and assignment: can they subcontract or assign to a third party without consent? Where is governing law/forum?
9. Produce the output: a table of clause, what it says in plain words, risk level, and the question to raise — then route flagged items to a qualified lawyer. This checklist does not replace legal advice.

## Checks

- Every money, exit, and data term captured in plain language.
- Uncertain items explicitly routed to counsel, not resolved by guesswork.
