# Professional Email Composer

Emails succeed when the reader knows within five seconds what happened and what they must do.

## Steps

1. Lead with the point: put the outcome, request, or headline in the first one or two sentences. Background comes after, never before.
2. Make the ask explicit and singular where possible: one bolded or clearly stated action with a deadline, e.g. "Please confirm budget approval by Friday 17:00".
3. Structure longer emails with short labelled sections: "Context", "What changed", "What I need from you".
4. Match formality to the relationship: mirror the other party's greetings and sign-offs; default to plain, warm, and specific over clever.
5. For difficult messages (delay, decline, bad news): acknowledge the impact in one line, state the fact without burying it, then give the reason and the recovery plan.
6. Keep the subject line informative: "Q3 report: 2-day delay, new ETA Oct 12" beats "Update".
7. Before sending, verify: does the email answer "what, so what, now what"? Is every attachment mentioned? Are names and dates correct?

## Pitfalls

- Multiple asks hidden in one paragraph — split or rank them.
- Apologizing twice; apologize once, then move to the plan.
