# Multi-Source Research Report Builder

The deliverable is a decision aid: every claim traceable to a source, every conflict surfaced, every gap admitted.

## Steps

1. Convert the question into research questions (3-5 specific, answerable sub-questions) and note who needs the answer by when. Broad questions produce broad mush.
2. Plan source diversity deliberately: at least 3 independent source types (official statistics, industry publications, practitioner blogs, primary documents). Two outlets republishing the same press release are one source.
3. For each source, record as you read: URL, publisher, date, and the specific claim it supports. Claims older than the relevant window get a date caveat.
4. Evaluate each source: primary > secondary; named author and date > anonymous; method stated > method implied. Note conflicts between sources explicitly — do not average away disagreement; report "A says X (2024 report), B says Y (vendor blog)".
5. Synthesize by sub-question: answer, evidence (2-3 strongest sources), confidence (high = multiple independent sources agree; medium = limited or mixed; low = single source or inference).
6. Label every inference: "we infer" versus "source states". Unlabeled inference is how rumors become facts in reports.
7. Close with: implications for the decision, what the research could not answer, and the 2-3 fastest ways to close the biggest gap (a specific dataset, an interview, a test).
8. Format: executive summary (5 lines), findings per sub-question with confidence tags, source list with dates, limitations.

## Checks

- No claim appears without a source or an inference label.
- Conflicts reported, not smoothed.
