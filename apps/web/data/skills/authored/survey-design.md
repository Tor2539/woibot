# Survey and Questionnaire Designer

Bad survey questions manufacture findings. Design the analysis before the questions.

## Steps

1. Define the decision the survey serves and the 3-5 metrics or cuts you will need from it (e.g. "satisfaction by plan tier", "top request themes"). Every question must map to one.
2. Choose the sample and note the bias risk: who is reachable vs who is excluded (only happy customers answer; only power users saw the feature). State the caveat that will travel with results.
3. Write questions one construct at a time: a question measures one thing. Split "Was the app fast and easy to use?" into two questions — double-barreled questions are unanalyzable.
4. Wording rules: no leading adjectives ("How excellent was..."), no negations ("Do you never..."), no jargon, no memory questions beyond ~2 weeks ("last time you used it" not "how often in general").
5. Scales: pick one polarity and stick to it across the survey (5-point, anchored with labels on endpoints); use NPS, CSAT, or star consistently — never mix 1-5 and 1-10 mid-survey.
6. Sequence: easy engaging questions first, sensitive ones last, one screen-break per section, and keep the whole thing under 5 minutes (state the time in the invite).
7. Add one open-ended question maximum ("what one thing should we improve?") and plan its analysis (tagging scheme with 5-8 codes).
8. Pilot with 5 people: check completion time, question misreads, and whether the analysis plan can actually be filled from the answers. Fix, then launch.

## Checks

- Analysis plan exists and every question feeds it.
- No leading, double-barreled, or negated items.
