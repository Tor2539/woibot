# Risk Register Builder

Vague risks get no plans. "Communication might be an issue" protects nothing; a register entry names cause, event, and consequence.

## Steps

1. Collect risks from the team, past project reports, and the plan's assumptions. Write each as a three-part statement: "Because [cause], [event] may occur, resulting in [consequence]". This structure forces specificity.
2. Score each risk: probability (low/med/high) and impact (low/med/high) against the project's objectives. Score impact in the units that matter — schedule days, cost, or scope loss — not adjectives alone.
3. Rank by a simple matrix; the top 5 get response plans now, the rest get monitoring triggers. A register with 40 equally-treated risks is a filing exercise, not management.
4. For top risks, choose a response type deliberately: avoid (change the plan), mitigate (reduce probability or impact), transfer (contract/insurance), or accept (with a stated trigger for re-review). Name the owner — risks without owners are wishes.
5. Add early-warning indicators per top risk: the measurable signal that the risk is materializing (vendor misses two milestones; defect rate trend; key-person overtime) and where that signal appears.
6. Note contingency: for the biggest schedule/cost risks, what pre-agreed action happens when the indicator fires (use buffer, drop scope item Y, activate alternate vendor).
7. Set the cadence: review the register in the regular project meeting (15 minutes: new risks, changed scores, fired indicators), and re-score monthly.
8. Retire risks with a note when they close; a register full of stale entries trains the team to ignore it.
