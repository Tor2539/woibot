# Cold Outreach Writer

Cold messages fail when they are about the sender. The recipient gives 10 seconds for one reason: relevance to their problem.

## Steps

1. Research the recipient for one specific, recent hook: a launch, job posting, funding, expansion, or a visible pain in their work. Generic flattery ("love your work") reads as a template in the first three words.
2. State why them, specifically, in one sentence: their situation + the gap you noticed. "You're hiring SDRs in Bangkok — scaling outbound usually hits reply-rate walls" beats any pitch deck opener.
3. Make the value proposition concrete and short: what changes for them, with a number or named mechanism. One sentence. If it needs a paragraph, it is not a cold email.
4. One low-friction ask: interest check, 15 minutes, or permission to send a 2-page case. Never a form, never "pick a time next week" in message one. End with a question that is easy to answer from a phone.
5. Keep it under 90 words total; no attachments in the first message (filters and etiquette).
6. Plan the follow-up sequence: 3 touches max over ~2 weeks; each follow-up adds something new (a relevant result, a useful observation) — "just bumping this" teaches them to ignore you. Final message: polite close-the-loop line; it often gets the reply.
7. Track what matters: reply rate by hook type and ask type; kill templates that go two sequences below reply threshold rather than sending harder.

## Checks

- Sender uses "you" more than "we/I".
- The ask can be answered in five words.
